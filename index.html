Quorum · Sovereign Chain Ltd

AI outputs are confident, well-formatted — and sometimes wrong. We catch the wrong ones.

Quorum is a multi-model verification layer: it fans a question across heterogeneous LLM families, scores their agreement and disagreement, and wraps the result in a pre-registered, mechanically-scored audit trail — designed to support EU AI Act (Article 14) human-oversight obligations.

What it does

  • Cross-family consensus — one query, many independent model families (API + local), semantic agreement scoring, per-model breakdown and cost caps.
  • Disagreement analytics — pairwise divergence matrices that show reviewers exactly where a model panel splits, and where a confident answer stands alone.
  • Pre-registered verdicts — evaluation criteria fixed before the data arrives; outcomes scored mechanically, not narratively.
  • Panel health monitoring — dead providers, degraded quorums and silent fallbacks are surfaced, not swallowed.

Proof, not promises

We stress-test Quorum in the most adversarial verification domain we could find: live, contested claims in frontier astrophysics — audited by multiple AI agents cross-checking each other, with every number traceable to a dated lab-notebook file. One week of operation produced, among others:

A factor-of-10 bug intercepted before submission An AI-drafted manuscript claimed its model beat the standard halo model “in 80% of 175 galaxies.” Independent audit located a factor-of-10 error in one line of the benchmark; corrected, the headline collapsed to a coin flip — before anything left the building.

Published statistics reproduced to the third decimal place The pipeline independently re-derived the central correlation statistic of a contested peer-reviewed result (r = 0.057 vs published 0.057) from raw public catalogues — without prior knowledge of the published value.
Case file: external-field-effect replication, Aug 2026
The auditor audited: a public, symmetric retraction When the lead auditing agent wrongly accused another agent of fabricating numbers, the trail showed the numbers were legitimate — and the auditor’s retraction entered the record with the same ink as everyone else’s errors. Verification systems that cannot indict themselves cannot be trusted to indict others.
